Part 04 — Integration Notes

Where this lives on the site.

Four placement decisions worth thinking through with Chip before any of this gets coded into the live site. The timeline is a real component — it should be embeddable. The story is editorial — it should breathe.

01

Place the backstory before the tier selector.

The current site jumps straight to plans. Move the story above the fold of "what you'll do together." A reader who finds themselves in the journey is a reader who is already pre-qualified for a tier — they just need to be told which one.

02

The timeline is a standalone asset.

Build it as a self-contained embeddable widget. Drop it into the site, drop it into a LinkedIn post as a screen recording, drop it into the front of a sales deck. One object, many surfaces. Same story everywhere.

03

Map the timeline eras directly to the tiers.

Each era on the timeline answers the question "is this you?" — and each one points at the NexGen tier built for shops at that stage. No tier-shopping anxiety. The reader sees themselves, then sees the obvious next step.

04

Record an audio version of your story.

Once the prompts are answered, record yourself reading the final draft over a low background of shop-floor ambient sound. Embed it on the page. Voice-of-Brenden carries credibility no transcript ever will — and it pre-sells the podcast that's already in the pipeline.

Tell the story once, tell it true. Then let the four pillars, the calculator, and the tiers do what they were designed to do.
