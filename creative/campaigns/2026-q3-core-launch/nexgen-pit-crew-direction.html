The Premise

You can't win a race by buying a better engine. You win it with a crew that knows the track.

The fastest cars on the grid aren't the ones with the most horsepower — they're the ones with the deepest pit crew, the sharpest race strategy, and the most disciplined setup. Most panel shops are trying to win on horsepower alone: bigger CNC, more software, more hires. The car keeps crashing into the same corner.

NexGen is the pit crew. We don't drive your car. We make sure the car wins.

"Machines alone aren't enough. Software without process creates struggle. Automation without standards creates chaos. The hardest part of modernization was never buying the technology. It was rebuilding the workflow."

DAY ONE · BUILT FROM ZERO TURN01

The Build

Brenden built ADS from the ground up — paper schematics, hand-drawn layouts, an owner who swept the floor at the end of every shift. He hired carefully, trained personally, and grew the shop one customer at a time. By the end of the first decade, the shop was running on senior wiremen who knew every panel by heart and customers who kept coming back. Working harder was the strategy. For a long while, it was enough.

"Working harder was the strategy. For a long while, it was enough."

EARLY WARNINGS · IGNORED TURN02

The First Cracks

A missed date. A senior wireman muttering about retirement. A regular customer placing a smaller order than last quarter. The first cracks are easy to miss — they look like having a bad month. Brenden chalked it up to volume, weather, the cycle. He worked harder.

"The first cracks are easy to miss. They look like 'just having a bad month.'"

2014 · SPS DRIVES · NUREMBERG TURN03

The Vision

Spring 2014. The panel building hall at SPS Drives. Software, automation, workflow, and manufacturing — operating as one connected system. No one fighting through a pile of prints. The future already existed. Most of us in the United States just hadn't seen it yet.

"He thought he'd found the lever. He hadn't. Not the whole one."

FIRST WRONG TURN · MACHINE BUY TURN04

The CNC Misjudgment

He came home convinced the answer was horsepower. ADS bought its first Steinhauer ModCenter CNC. Precision arrived almost overnight — the kind of repeatability the shop had never delivered before. He thought he'd found it. He'd found a piece of it.

"Precision wasn't the problem. The strategy was."

PACE DROP · +10–20% / LAP TURN05

The Gradient

For two-and-a-half years, quality went up and throughput went up — and the bids kept coming back ten to twenty percent under. Competitors weren't cutting corners. Their lap times were genuinely faster. They had a better setup, a better strategy, a better crew.

"Their margins were holding while their prices were dropping."

PIT FAILURE · × N TURN06

The Wrong Answers

More automation. Consultants who left binders. Higher-skilled engineers. Three salespeople in two years. Longer hours behind the wheel. Heavier reliance on the senior wiremen who carried the entire production capability of the company in their heads.

"None of it moved the needle by a single point."

REGROUP · ORDER OF OPS TURN07

The Real Strategy

It wasn't a part. It was an order of operations. Lean — see the waste. Standards — keep what works. Cell-based production — flow the car cleanly through the garage. Employee engagement — the crew has to own the car. Then EPLAN started to land. Then automation paid off.

"The goal was never to remove craftsmanship. The goal was to remove struggle."

CHECKERED FLAG · +70% PACE TURN08

Championship Pace

Today ADS runs 70% faster than it did. Not because the driver works harder — because the crew works better. Engineering flows into manufacturing. Information moves with clarity. Good people freed from broken processes return to craftsmanship. And every few months, another owner shows up asking "who's your crew?"
